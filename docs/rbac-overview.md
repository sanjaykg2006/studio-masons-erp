# Who-Can-Do-What: A Plain-English Guide

*A short guide to how access works in the Studio-Masons system. No technical
knowledge needed.*

---

## The idea in one line

Everyone signs in to the same system, but each person only sees and can touch the
things their **role** allows. This keeps work tidy and private by default —
nothing is shared unless someone is meant to have it.

---

## First: how people get in

The system is **invite-only**. Nobody can sign themselves up. Someone in charge
sends you an invite by email; you click the link and you're in. If you leave,
your access is removed and you can no longer sign in.

---

## The three "doors"

Access is split into three simple, separate areas. Think of them as three doors,
each doing one job. They never interfere with each other.

### Door 1 — Access Control (the company / HR door)
This is the company-wide setup, used by an administrator. From here they:
- **invite new people** and remove people who leave,
- give each person an **office job title** (a ready-made bundle that decides which
  back-office screens they can open — e.g. Dashboard, Finance),
- **create departments** (Design, Project Management, etc.),
- choose **who leads** each department,
- choose **which tools** each department gets.

This door is only about the company and the back office — it doesn't touch the
work inside any single project.

### Door 2 — People & Access (each department's own door)
Every department has its own **People & Access** screen, run by that department's
lead (or an admin). This is the day-to-day "who's on my team and what's their
role" screen. In one place you can:
- **add or remove** people on the team,
- give a person a **role across the whole department** (e.g. make someone a
  "Designer" on every project at once),
- put people into **sub-teams** — for Design, that's **Concept** and **Technical**,
  which keep their tasks private from each other,
- tick a few **extra abilities** (like "can create projects" or "can manage the
  template library").

You reach it by opening a department (e.g. **Design → People & Access**).

### Door 3 — Project roles (inside each project)
Inside any single project, whoever runs it can add people and give each a
**project role** (Project Lead, Designer, Junior, Vendor, and so on). That role
decides what the person can do **on that one project only**. The same person can
be a Lead on one project and a Designer on another.

Remove someone from a project and they instantly lose access to it.

---

## What a "role" actually is

A role is just a **ready-made bundle of permissions** — a tidy list of what a
person is allowed to do. Change the bundle once and everyone with that role
updates automatically. This is easier and safer than setting permissions for each
person one by one.

---

## Two rules that keep it safe

1. **Nothing is allowed unless it's been granted.** If a permission hasn't been
   ticked, the answer is "no." There are no accidental open doors.
2. **The rules are enforced deep in the system, not just on screen.** Even if
   someone tried to go around the buttons, the database itself refuses anything
   they're not allowed to do.

---

## A quick example

Say Priya joins the Design team as a designer:
1. **Access Control:** an admin invites Priya and gives her an office job title.
2. **Design → People & Access:** the Design lead adds Priya to the team, makes her
   a "Designer" across the department, and puts her in the **Concept** sub-team.
3. **A specific project:** if she should also help on one particular project with
   a different role, the project's owner adds her there too.

That's the whole picture: **company setup, then department people, then project
roles.**
